# 2. Historical / constitutional background

## 2.1 Why the Act was introduced

The scanned material begins by placing Karnataka's cooperative law in the broader development of cooperative legislation in India. It refers to earlier cooperative legislation, including the **Co-operative Credit Societies Act, 1904**, the **Co-operative Societies Act, 1912**, the **Government of India Act, 1919**, and the provincial/state cooperative laws that followed.

The material then explains that, before the 1959 law, different areas of the then Mysore State were governed by different cooperative laws. The objective of the new legislation was therefore to establish a more uniform cooperative framework.

The Act is **Karnataka Act No. 11 of 1959**. The statutory history also records that the Mysore Co-operative Societies Act, 1959 came into force on **1 July 1960**.

> [!tip] Exam memory
> **Act = 1959; Rules = 1960; commencement of the Mysore Act = 1 July 1960.**

# 3. Registration and the legal identity of a cooperative society

## 3.1 Section 6 — Registration

### Core idea
A cooperative society must follow the statutory registration process. The Rules prescribe the application procedure and forms.

### Rule 3 — Application for Registration and Procedure

The Rules provide that an application for registration under Section 6 must contain the prescribed particulars and be in the prescribed form (Schedule A in the statutory Rules). The application also identifies an applicant to whom communications may be sent.

### Exam pointer

> **Section 6 = statutory registration requirement.**
>
> **Rule 3 = practical application/procedure.**

---

## 3.2 Section 9 — Effect of registration

Registration gives the society the statutory character of a body corporate, with continuing legal existence and the capacity to hold property, enter contracts and sue/be sued in its registered name.

> [!question] Quick recall
> What is the practical significance of registration?
>
> **Answer:** It gives the registered society a separate statutory corporate identity and legal capacity.

# 4. Bye-laws

## 4.1 Section 12 — Amendment of bye-laws

The Act regulates how the bye-laws of a registered cooperative society may be amended.

## 4.2 Section 13 — When amendments come into force

Unless an amendment specifies a different operative date, an amendment to the bye-laws generally comes into force when it is registered, subject to the Act's appellate provisions.

### Rule 5 — Subject-matter of bye-laws

The Rules require the bye-laws to deal with core organisational matters, including:

1. Name and address of the society.
2. Area of operation.
3. Objects of the society.
4. Method of raising funds and maximum shareholding of an individual member.
5. Nature and extent of members' liability.
6. Borrowing limits and interest.
7. Entrance and other fees.
8. Purposes for which funds may be used.
9. Admission conditions and members' rights/liabilities.
10. Meetings and notices.
11. Appointment/removal of committee and officers; their powers, duties and terms.
12. Disposal of net profits.
13. Preparation/submission/publication of annual statements.
14. Custody/investment of funds and accounting.
15. Required special funds.
16. Credit-society matters such as maximum loans, interest, loan conditions, procedure, defaults and recall.
17. Non-credit society business procedures, stock-taking and responsibility for stock.

The Rules also permit bye-laws to address matters such as withdrawal from membership, death/ineligibility, transfer of shares/interests, appropriation of member payments, authorisation of officers for litigation, employee recruitment/service/discipline, and affiliation with supervising/financing institutions.

### Rule 6 — Amendment procedure

A proposed bye-law amendment generally requires:

- a resolution passed by the prescribed majority at a general meeting;
- proper notice to members;
- application to the Registrar with the required supporting documents;
- submission within the prescribed period (the current Rules text commonly specifies **60 days**, subject to condonation for sufficient cause).

> [!warning] Act vs Rule trap
> **Section 12** tells you about the statutory amendment framework.
>
> **Rule 6** tells you the procedural/documentary route for implementing an amendment.

# 5. Amalgamation, transfer of assets/liabilities and division

## Section 14 — Amalgamation, transfer of assets and liabilities and division

This is one of the most important provisions in the scanned material.

A cooperative society may, by a resolution passed by a **two-thirds majority of members present and voting** at a general meeting:

- divide itself into two or more cooperative societies; or
- transfer all or part of its assets and liabilities to another cooperative society which agrees to the transfer by a like resolution.

Two or more societies may similarly amalgamate and form a new cooperative society.

### Registrar approval

The resolution must contain the particulars of the proposed transfer/division/amalgamation and **does not take effect merely because the general body passed it**. Registrar approval is required.

For a cooperative bank, the statutory text contains an additional safeguard involving prior written sanction of the Reserve Bank in the circumstances specified by the Act.

### Protection of creditors and dissenting members

After approval, notice is given to creditors and members who did not vote in favour. They receive a statutory opportunity to withdraw their shares/deposits/loans within the prescribed one-month period.

A member/creditor who does not exercise the option within that period is deemed to have assented.

The resolution does not take effect until either:

1. the required assent has been obtained/deemed obtained; or
2. the claims of those exercising the withdrawal option have been met in full.

### Conveyance effect

Where the resolution transfers assets/liabilities, the resolution itself operates as a sufficient conveyance for vesting those assets/liabilities in the transferee, subject to the statutory scheme.

> [!tip] Memory formula
> **Section 14 = 2/3 + Registrar + notice + 1 month + claims settled.**

# 6. Membership

## Section 16 — Persons who may become members

The current statutory text has evolved through amendments. The scan's heading is correctly identified as **Section 16 — Persons who may become members**.

The provision covers categories such as:

- eligible individuals;
- other cooperative societies;
- State/Central Government;
- specified institutions;
- firms, companies and other bodies corporate covered by the provision;
- specified market institutions; and
- local authorities,

subject to the version of the Act applicable at the relevant date.

The modern amended wording also contains additional conditions for an individual member, including the requirements introduced by later amendments concerning the society's services and area of operation, and the Act contains a separate reference to depositors.

### Admission procedure

The Act requires an application in writing. The society must deal with the application within the statutory period applicable under the current text.

> [!warning] Current-law caution
> Older study material may state a different admission period because Section 16 has been amended. Always check the version applicable to the question. Do **not** memorise an old two-/three-month figure without checking the amendment version.

---

## Section 17 — Disqualification for membership

Important disqualifications include:

1. Insolvency-related disqualification.
2. Certain criminal convictions, subject to the statutory exceptions and cooling-off period.
3. Carrying on the same kind of business as the cooperative society, as covered by the provision.
4. Existing membership in another society carrying on the same kind of business, as covered by the provision.

If an existing member becomes subject to a statutory disqualification, membership can cease from the date the disqualification is incurred.

The Act also contains a **three-year obligation/continuity rule**: failure to fulfil membership obligations under the Act, Rules or bye-laws for the continuous statutory period can result in cessation of membership.

### Registrar's role

Where a question arises whether a person has ceased to be a member, the Registrar can decide the matter after giving the person an opportunity of being heard.

> [!tip] Distinguish
> **Section 17 = disqualification from membership.**
>
> **Section 29C = disqualification for being a member of the committee.**

# 7. Nominal / associate membership

## Section 18

The Act permits a cooperative society to admit specified persons/entities as nominal or associate members, subject to the statutory conditions.

The important conceptual distinction is:

- **ordinary member** → full membership rights as provided by the Act/rules/bye-laws;
- **nominal/associate member** → rights are restricted by the Act and bye-laws and may not include voting or office-bearing rights.

---

# 8. Rights of members and voting

## Section 19 — Rights only after due payment / acquisition of interest

A member generally cannot exercise membership rights until the required payment or interest acquisition specified in the Act/bye-laws has been satisfied, subject to statutory exceptions.

## Section 20 — Votes of members

### Core rule
The general principle is:

> **One member = one vote.**

The Act also identifies categories that do not have voting rights in specified circumstances, including nominal/associate members and other statutory categories.

### Exam trap

Do not automatically equate:

- **membership**,
- **voting membership**, and
- **committee eligibility**.

They are separate statutory questions.

# 9. Annual General Meeting

## Section 27 — Annual General Meeting

Every cooperative society must convene the annual general meeting once a year within the statutory time-frame, subject to the Registrar's power to extend the period as permitted by the Act.

The AGM deals with, among other matters:

1. Annual report.
2. Latest available audit report and committee/board response.
3. Inquiry report, if any.
4. Disposal of net profits.
5. Operational deficit and reduction programme.
6. Programme of activities for the ensuing year.
7. Annual budget.
8. Creation of specific reserve and other funds.
9. Membership of the society in other cooperative societies.
10. Annual report/accounts of subsidiary organisation, if any.
11. List of employees recruited who are relatives of specified committee/Chief Executive persons.
12. Bye-law amendments.
13. Code of conduct.
14. Admission and termination of members.
15. Other matters permitted by the bye-laws.

### Failure to call AGM

The Act provides consequences for responsible office bearers/committee members and employees, and permits the Registrar or authorised person to convene the meeting in the circumstances specified.

> [!tip] AGM mnemonic
> **A-A-I-P-D-P-B-F-M-S-E-B-C-M-O**
>
> Annual report → Audit → Inquiry → Profits → Deficit → Programme → Budget → Funds → Membership → Subsidiary → Employees → Bye-laws → Code → Membership changes → Other.

# 10. Special General Meeting

## Section 28 — Special General Meeting

The committee may call a special general meeting at any time.

It must also call one within the statutory period when a proper requisition is received from:

- the Registrar; or
- the prescribed number/proportion of members.

If the committee fails to call it, the Registrar or authorised person may call the meeting.

> **AGM = periodic statutory meeting.**
>
> **SGM = issue-specific meeting capable of being requisitioned.**

# 11. Management of the society

## Section 28A — Management vested in the committee

The management of a cooperative society vests in a committee constituted under:

- the Act,
- the Rules, and
- the bye-laws.

The committee exercises the powers and performs the duties/functions conferred or imposed by those instruments.

### Committee size — exam-oriented snapshot

The Act provides different maximum sizes according to the type and geographical area of operation of the society. The statutory text includes, for example:

- primary/secondary society operating in part of a taluk → **9**;
- whole taluk → **11**;
- beyond taluk but not beyond district → **13**;
- beyond district → **18**;
- specified federal society → **21**;
- apex society → **32**.

There are statutory provisions for reservation in primary societies, including seats for Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes and women, subject to the conditions of the Act.

### Term

The statutory framework provides a **five cooperative-year** term for committee members under the applicable provisions, with special transitional/first-committee provisions.

### Secret ballot

Committee members elect office bearers from among themselves, and the statutory provision requires secret ballot.

---

# 12. Rule 13 onwards — Management and elections

The Rules supplement the Act by prescribing procedures for:

- election of committee members;
- identity cards;
- election procedure;
- election of office bearers;
- first committee after registration;
- first office-bearer election;
- remuneration/conditions relating to administrators/special officers;
- representative general bodies and electoral colleges where applicable.

### Rule 12A / 12B — Representative General Body

These provisions are relevant where a society has a representative general body. Rule 12B deals with the number of representatives and their election, with different arrangements depending on the society's area of operation.

> [!warning] Section vs Rule
> A question saying **"who has the power?"** may be testing the Act.
>
> A question saying **"what is the procedure/form/notice?"** may be testing the Rules.

# 13. Committee disqualification

## Section 29C — Disqualification for committee membership

This is broader and more specific than Section 17.

The provision covers, among other grounds:

- default to a cooperative society;
- financial interest in specified contracts/transactions;
- prohibited business connections;
- certain legal-practitioner relationships;
- specified employment relationships;
- certain convictions;
- corrupt-practice findings;
- failure to remit amounts as required;
- representation of a society that is itself in default;
- failure to arrange elections within the statutory period;
- loss of the representative relationship in a higher-tier society.

The Registrar decides questions of disqualification after giving the affected person an opportunity of being heard.

The Act also empowers removal/disqualification in specified circumstances where a committee member acts fraudulently, with gross negligence, contrary to the Act/Rules/bye-laws, contrary to authorised resolutions, or prejudicially to the society's interests.

# 14. Privileges of cooperative societies

## Chapter V — Sections 32 onwards

### Section 32 — First charge on certain assets

A cooperative society can have a **first charge** on specified assets of a member/past/deceased member for debts/outstanding demands, subject to statutory priority rules.

The provision covers, among other things:

- crops and agricultural produce;
- cattle/fodder;
- agricultural or industrial implements/machinery;
- raw materials;
- finished products.

A property subject to the charge generally cannot be transferred contrary to the statutory protection without the required written permission; an unauthorised transfer is rendered void under the provision.

### Section 33 — Charge on land in specified loan situations

For specified cooperative loans, a member may be required to make a declaration creating a charge over land or the member's interest in land.

The Act regulates alienation, release of part of the charge after repayment, priority and recording of the charge.

# 16. Properties and Funds

## Section 56 — Funds not to be divided

The basic rule is:

> Funds of a cooperative society other than net profits cannot simply be distributed among members as bonus/dividend or otherwise.

A member may receive remuneration, allowances or honorarium for services rendered, within the statutory/bye-law framework.

The Act also restricts allowances/fees payable for committee functions to the prescribed maximum.

# 17. Net profits and their disposal

## Section 57

### Reserve fund — minimum 25%

A cooperative society must transfer **not less than 25% of its net profits** to the reserve fund.

### Cooperative Education Fund — 2%

From the balance of net profits, the society is required to contribute **2%** to the Cooperative Education Fund, subject to the current statutory wording.

Failure to make the required contribution can affect the society's ability to pay dividend as specified by the Act.

### Other uses of balance

The balance may be used for purposes authorised by the Act, including:

- member bonus based on business done, subject to restrictions applicable to credit societies;
- specified special funds;
- charitable donations within the statutory ceiling;
- employee bonus subject to the statutory limit.

> [!tip] Numbers to memorise
> **25% → Reserve Fund**
>
> **2% → Cooperative Education Fund**
>
> **10% → statutory ceiling for the specified charitable donation category**
>
> **2 months' pay → ceiling for employee bonus in the provision's stated context**

# 18. Section 57A — Cooperative Education and Training

The Cooperative Education Fund is intended for:

- promotion of the cooperative movement;
- education of members;
- education of directors/cooperators;
- education of the general public;
- training of cooperative-society employees.

The statutory provision also deals with:

- payment of contributions to the Karnataka State Cooperative Federation;
- credits to the fund, including government grants/donations as specified;
- administration of the fund;
- advisory-committee approval for expenditure as prescribed.

> [!question] Recall
> What is the purpose of Section 57A?
>
> **Answer:** Cooperative education + training + promotion of the cooperative movement.

# 19. Section 58 — Investment of funds

A cooperative society may invest/deposit funds only through the channels permitted by the Act, including specified government savings/securities, cooperative institutions/banks and other statutory avenues subject to the conditions in the Act.

> **Exam focus:** This is not a general corporate freedom to invest anywhere. The Act controls the permitted investment channels.

### Section 61 — Transactions with non-members

# 21. Audit

## Section 63 — Audit

The Director of Cooperative Audit is responsible for auditing, or causing the accounts to be audited by an authorised person, at least once in each year.

The audit is not merely arithmetic.

It includes examination of:

- overdue debts;
- cash balance;
- securities;
- valuation of assets and liabilities;
- working of the society; and
- other prescribed particulars.

The auditor/authorised person has statutory access to:

- books;
- accounts;
- documents;
- papers;
- securities;
- cash;
- other society property.

The auditor can also summon a person responsible for custody of these materials to produce them at the specified public office/headquarters/branch.

### Rule 30 — Audit fee/remuneration

Rule 30 deals with audit fees/remuneration and the scale applicable to cooperative societies, including the role of the Director of Cooperative Audit and the Government's approval framework.

### Rules 51–53 — Accounts and returns

These Rules are highly relevant for exam questions on the **procedural side of audit**:

- **Rule 51:** accounts and books to be maintained.
- **Rule 52:** power to direct accounts/books to be written up.
- **Rule 53:** statements and returns to be furnished.

Rule 53 covers annual financial statements/returns such as:

- receipts and disbursements;
- profit and loss account;
- balance sheet;
- other statements/returns prescribed by the competent audit authority.

> [!tip] One-line distinction
> **Section 63 = legal audit duty and scope.**
>
> **Rule 30 = audit fee/remuneration framework.**
>
> **Rules 51–53 = books, accounts and returns.**

# 22. Inquiry

## Section 64 — Inquiry by Registrar

The Registrar may, on his own motion, conduct or authorise an inquiry into a matter touching the:

- constitution;
- working; or
- financial condition

of a cooperative society.

### Inquiry on application

An inquiry can also be triggered by application from:

1. a cooperative society to which the concerned society is affiliated;
2. a majority of the committee; or
3. not less than **one-third of the total members** of the society.

### Time-limit

The statutory framework provides for completion within **12 months**, with a possible further extension of **6 months** for recorded reasons, subject to the wording/applicability of the current Act.

### Powers during inquiry

The Registrar/authorised person can:

- access books/accounts/documents/securities/cash/property;
- summon persons responsible for custody of records;
- summon persons believed to know about the society's affairs;
- examine persons on oath;
- exercise the other statutory powers specified in the provision.

> [!tip] Number
> **Inquiry trigger by members = 1/3.**
>
> **Normal statutory completion period = 12 months.**
>
> **Possible extension = 6 months with recorded reasons.**

# 23. Inspection

## Section 65 — Inspection of books

Inspection is distinct from a full inquiry.

### Think of the distinction this way

- **Audit (63):** recurring statutory examination of accounts.
- **Inquiry (64):** investigation into constitution/working/financial condition.
- **Inspection (65):** statutory inspection of books/records.
- **Surcharge (69):** financial liability mechanism for loss caused by specified conduct.

---

# 24. Surcharge

## Section 69 — Surcharge

The surcharge mechanism is intended to make responsible persons financially answerable where statutory conditions establish loss/damage caused through specified misconduct, negligence, misapplication or related conduct.

### Exam concept

> **Audit finds/records financial irregularities.**
>
> **Inquiry investigates.**
>
> **Surcharge can convert statutory responsibility into a financial liability/order.**
